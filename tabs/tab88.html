The sample is drawn round-robin across objects, not off the top of the queue: twenty fields from a single object is the case a model handles best, so a sample built that way would flatter the result you are about to trust. Read the twenty sentences, adjust the writing rules, then let the run go.
Addressable excludes managed-package fields: about four fifths of the custom fields in a mature org belong to a package and cannot be edited. Counting them would make the debt look larger and the progress impossible.

Fields grouped into business domains, so the org can be read by subject rather than by object. A domain is proposed with a name, a definition and a verdict — coherent, catch-all, or merges two subjects.

Validating a domain improves the prompt of every field in it — the neighbours a field keeps are part of its grounding. A catch-all domain therefore harms more than it helps, which is why the verdict comes before the assignments.
A decision goes stale when its evidence changes, not when its subject does. Measured on real orgs, the difference is a factor of 15 at ninety days: what moves is the Apex class the decision was read from, not the field itself.

★ Key features

  • Ranks fields by what they drive — automations, UI placements, exposure — not by name.
  • Trial run on 20 fields, sampled across objects, before committing to a bulk run.
  • Prompts are grounded in real evidence: writers, readers, and file:line code extracts.
  • Sensitivity classification (GDPR, PCI) with propagation across naming families.
  • Org glossary of acronyms, business domains, and a re-confirmation sweep when evidence moves.
  • Round trip through Excel, then a deployable metadata package for a sandbox.
