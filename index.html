Built to be trusted

Autonomous by design.

Calumet is a program you run, not a service you sign up for. Here is what that means.

📦

Nothing to install

Unzip and run. Calumet ships with its own Java runtime, so there is no Java to set up and no admin rights to ask for.

🔑

Your rights, nothing more

You sign in with your own Salesforce user, like the sf CLI. Calumet can do what that user can do — and nothing else.

🏠

No Calumet server

Everything runs locally on your computer, like the sf CLI. No account, no cloud, no telemetry: Calumet talks to your Salesforce orgs, and to GitHub to look for updates.

🔒

Tokens stay on your PC

Connections use a Connected App / External Client App. Tokens are encrypted on your machine with a master password that is never sent anywhere.

Who talks to whom

Your machine, your orgs. That’s it.

There is no Calumet cloud in the middle. Your data goes from your Salesforce org to your computer, and nowhere else.

Your data and credentialsUpdate check only
How it connects

The same login you already use.

Calumet never asks for your Salesforce password. It uses the standard OAuth flow, through a Connected App / External Client App in your org.

You sign in

Calumet opens your browser on the usual Salesforce login page. Your password and MFA stay between you and Salesforce.

Salesforce hands back a token

The token carries your own permissions. If your user cannot delete a field, neither can Calumet.

The token is locked on your PC

It is encrypted with a master password you choose. There is no recovery and nothing is sent anywhere.

You stay in control. Revoke Calumet’s access at any time from Salesforce (Setup → Connected Apps OAuth Usage), or remove the org in Calumet.

⚠ macOS — first launch

Calumet is not signed with an Apple developer certificate yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. Move CalumetTool to Applications, then right-click it (or Control-click) and choose Open, and confirm Open. You only need to do this once.

On macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later: double-click Calumet once, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway. Terminal alternative: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/CalumetTool.app

Questions

Is it safe?

Does Calumet send my data anywhere?

No. Your data goes from your Salesforce org to your computer. Calumet has no server of its own and collects no usage data. The only other contact is GitHub, to check whether a new version exists. (This website, which is separate from the application, counts visits without cookies.)

What can Calumet do in my org?

Exactly what the user you sign in with can do — the same as the Salesforce CLI. Use a user with the permissions you want Calumet to have.

How are my credentials stored?

Salesforce tokens are encrypted on your machine, under a master password you choose on first launch. The password is never sent anywhere; there is no recovery, only a reset that wipes the stored secrets.

Do I need to create something in Salesforce?

Sign-in goes through a Connected App / External Client App. Your Salesforce admin can register and control their own, and Calumet keeps it in a local vault on your machine.

Is it really free?

Yes. Everything you can use in Calumet today is free, and what is free today will stay free.

One limit exists today: DataSeeding imports up to 10,000 records per run. If you need more, ask for a free license by opening an issue on GitHub.

We are studying a few new, paid features for the future. The dependency graph is an example. They are not part of the free version, and they will be offered as additions: nothing you already use will be taken away.
